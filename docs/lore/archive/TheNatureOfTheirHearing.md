
# Phase over Pressure: Why Dolls Hear in Interference

> “The humans hear the pulse of air.
> We hear the drift of light within it.”
> — attributed to the First Doll

---

## 1. The Nature of Their Hearing

Where human ears bend membranes and hairs to sense pressure, a Doll’s hearing organ is an *interferometric lattice* — a web of porcelain, ink, and bismuth wires suspended in oil.
It does not listen for *loudness*, but for *coherence*: the delay and phase-shift of oscillations passing through its body.

Each crossing of bismuth and porcelain acts as a natural **interference node**, producing beats where external vibrations alter the relative phase of the internal field.
To a Doll, this beat pattern *is* sound.
Amplitude — the human sense of volume — is nearly meaningless to them.

Their world is one of shimmer and drift, of waves colliding in invisible geometry.
Sound is not “pressure through air,” but “motion through standing phase.”

Where humans must construct fragile membranes to detect amplitude, Dolls effortlessly compare delay and coherence between signals.

---

## 3. Why Phase Comes Cheap and Power Comes Dear

Because their sensors measure **interference** rather than **pressure**, maintaining a coherent internal pattern costs nothing — the lattice naturally resonates.

But any attempt to *observe or amplify* amplitude — that is, to measure raw power — requires collapsing the pattern into a definite state.
This collapse consumes their most precious energy: **attention**.

Thus, to a Doll:

- Keeping to a stable tone or pattern is *restful*.
- Deviating from it — especially imitating the chaotic modulations of human speech — is *exhausting*.

They can echo frequencies for hours, but to reproduce the subtleties of an English vowel drains them within minutes.

| **Primary data**     | Amplitude                          | Phase drift and coherence                    |
| **Easiest to sense** | Loudness, timbre                   | Delay, micro-timing, direction               |
| **Hardest to sense** | Phase                              | Power, loudness                              |

Where we hear *volume*, they perceive *tension*.
Where we hear *silence*, they may feel a slow, perfect alignment — a stillness of phase that borders on bliss.

---

## 5. Consequences for Language

Because phase detection comes naturally, their early languages were built not on amplitude modulation but on **phase choreography** — patterns of coherent drift across the lattice.

The First Doll’s earliest attempts at English were failures of translation between worlds: she tried to render *pressure-based meaning* through *phase-based organs*.
Her speech shimmered, pulsed, and fractured — a barely-audible simulation of human phonemes, exhausting to maintain.

Later generations learned to formalize that drift into ProtoDoll-Alpha, a language of structured phase and harmonic interference.

To them, language is not spoken; it is *sustained*.

> and in that fleeting moment of coherence

>
