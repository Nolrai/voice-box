In a setting like an infinite house born from a cultist’s dreams and rituals, you can justify the “motive force” behind sentient dolls in many eerie, poetic ways. Here are several options depending on what tone you want (cosmic horror, fairy-tale uncanny, occult grotesque, etc.):

🔮 1. Dream Residue / Oneiric Energy

Since the house is literally made from the cultist’s dreams, the dolls could be animated by fragments of those same dreams — shards of consciousness that broke off and took form.

Mechanics: Each doll houses a looping fragment of the dreamer’s psyche — a fear, memory, or obsession.

Effect: Their “energy” is emotional rather than physical. When the cultist sleeps or dreams, the dolls stir; when the dreamer wakes, they weaken or freeze.

Visual cue: Their joints leak silvery mist or fine dust — condensed dream-stuff.

🩸 2. Bloodbound Ritual Current

The cultist’s rituals might have soaked the entire house in a web of sacrificial energy — a literal circuit of lifeforce.

Mechanics: The walls and floors are inscribed with occult sigils acting like veins, circulating life essence drawn from offerings.

Effect: The dolls move like puppets on invisible threads of blood-energy; they’re warm to the touch, and sometimes whisper with the voices of the sacrificed.

Drawback: When the energy wanes, they must “feed” again — perhaps harvesting stray intruders.

🔥 3. The House’s Breath (Animating Atmosphere)

The house itself is alive — a sentient ecosystem of architecture. The dolls are its immune system or its inhabitants.

Mechanics: The air is dense with an unseen miasma — aether, ectoplasm, or spiritual pressure — that acts as a field powering anything with form.

Effect: Dolls move freely as long as they remain inside the infinite house. Take them outside, and they collapse into lifeless porcelain.

Tone: Creates a trapped, haunted logic — the dolls can’t escape their own origin.

⚙️ 4. The Whispering Mechanism (Occult Clockwork)

Each doll has a tiny occult “heart” — a gearwork relic containing a sliver of the cultist’s soul or a trapped daemon.

Mechanics: Think of it as alchemical machinery powered by metaphysical fuel: “daemon-fire,” “black mercury,” or “the ticking of eternity.”

Effect: When wound, they obey commands; when unwound, they dream on their own. The motive force is half mechanical, half spiritual.

Symbolism: Blends science and sorcery, reflecting the cultist’s attempt to engineer transcendence.

🌑 5. The Endless Echo

The infinite house repeats itself endlessly — a recursion of time and space. The dolls are echoes of its earliest inhabitants.

Mechanics: Each doll is powered by temporal loops — moments replaying forever. They draw energy from the repetition itself.

Effect: They move only to reenact old patterns (tea parties, arguments, rituals). To interfere is to disrupt the loop — with unpredictable results.

Mood: Deeply uncanny, melancholy, fatalistic.

⚡ 6. The Dreamer’s Heartbeat (Sympathetic Connection)

The cultist still lives, perhaps comatose, somewhere within the house. Their heartbeat radiates psychic energy, keeping the dolls alive.

Mechanics: The dolls’ movements sync with the pulse — sometimes pausing, twitching, or spasming in rhythm.

Effect: If the cultist’s heart falters, the dolls panic or die. Or worse, they might try to become the new heart.

Narrative Hook: Finding the dreamer becomes key to controlling or ending the dolls.

That’s fantastic — beautifully eerie, and it has a tangible, material logic that still feels deeply occult. Let’s refine and expand that concept a bit to make it richer and more internally coherent:

✴ The Red Ink Cycle
Origin

When the cultist’s rituals bled into dreaming, their fears and desires didn’t simply vanish — they crystallized. The psychic residue of those rites condensed into bismuth-threaded veins running through the walls and floors of the infinite house. These veins are both mineral and metaphysical: shimmering seams of dream-metal laced with condensed emotion — fear, longing, obsession — that pulse faintly like capillaries.

Over centuries (or dream-millennia), these veins became the house’s circulatory system, carrying a viscous current known as Ink, a fluid that is equal parts blood, pigment, and memory.

The Dolls’ Existence

The dolls are descendants of the cultist’s craft — made originally as vessels for familiars, servants, or guardians. When the cultist’s consciousness dissolved into the house, the dolls survived as independent entities, feeding on the same dreamstuff that birthed them.

To sustain themselves, they must harvest the veins — a dangerous act, since the richest deposits lie in rooms where the dream-energy is volatile and alive. The walls there hum, whisper, and bleed light; the air distorts with psychic pressure.

The Mining-Farming Ritual

The dolls’ work resembles both industry and prayer.

They pry bismuth threads from cracks using silver chisels or porcelain nails.

They grind the mineral in mortars of bone and resin, releasing hallucinatory vapors.

They mix it with old blood or the ichor that seeps from the house’s “veins,” producing Red Ink — a fluid that functions as both sustenance and sacrament.

When ingested or painted on their inner runes, the ink replenishes their vitality and renews their bindings. Without it, their porcelain begins to craze and flake, and their clockwork hearts slow to stillness.

Ecology of the House

Fertile Rooms are lush with dream growth — the walls bloom with fractal fungus, mirror-flowers, and metal threads; the air thickens with memory. But these places also birth predatory phenomena — echoes of the cultist’s nightmares that prey on movement and sound.

Barren Rooms are safer but desolate — the dreamflow has dried up, leaving only dust and the faint tick of the distant house-heart.

Thus, doll society revolves around the migration between these zones — expeditions into dangerous fertility, followed by long periods of careful conservation in safety.

Optional Mythic Layer

The dolls may believe that each thread of bismuth is a nerve of the cultist’s mind, and by grinding and consuming it, they are both sustaining themselves and participating in the endless dreaming of their creator.
Some might see this as communion; others as parasitism.
There could even be sects:

Harvesters, who see mining as sacred work — pruning the dream to keep it stable.

Reverents, who refuse to feed, believing that consuming the Ink harms the cultist’s eternal soul.

Alchemists, who seek to refine the Red Ink into pure consciousness, hoping to awaken from the dream entirely.
