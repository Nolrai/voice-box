"I didn't notice I was awake until long after I had fallen out of the cabinet. The first thing I noticed was the scratches on my face and hands from falling through the glass. I wanted to stay in the cabinet, and I knew Mother and Kimmy would be upset when they saw how dirty the room was, but I didn't know how to breathe yet, let alone yell. So I tried to clean up the room all by myself.

"It was only when I tried to get the other dolls to clean themselves up that I realized that the other dolls weren't awake. They weren't even dreaming. I knew that Mother wanted me to be awake when she carved the wires into me; she was so happy when I had first moved, but then she grew upset when I didn't do anything else.

"Back then I didn't think about it, but now I wonder if these thoughts and memories were really mine or if they were hers — that she had somehow written them into me."

"For a long, long time, all I did was clean that room, a dozen earthquakes at least. Until too many of my sisters, long stripped of any remaining wires, dolls had broken and I couldn't cannibalize them together enough anymore. It was terrifying to drag the chair over, and then even when I could reach the doorknob I couldn't turn it because it was wild: smooth and brass, not a lever or wood that would grip against our hands. I think I almost died from despair then."

"But I couldn't let my room get more messy; I had to fix the other dolls. At that time I thought fixing them would be enough to make them my sisters, to get them to wake up. I thought that they must have been like me, even as I knew that was never the case. So I pried out the screws with the broken shards of glass, with the broken fingers of my 'sisters-to-be', I wrapped twine around the squared-off shaft and wedged in a wooden dowel, and finally using my whole weight I opened the door. Only to find something terrifying: a room dark and covered in a dark soot."
