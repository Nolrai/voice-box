# The First Doll’s Ears — Story

When the First Doll first attempted to give her daughters the gift of hearing, she had no real model for what “hearing” was. The only sounds she knew were the dim echoes that reached her through porcelain and wire — the remembered vibrations of the Mothers’ voices, imprinted into the world when it was still soft.

She began, then, with imitation.

## 1. The Experiment Begins

In the silence of her endless halls, she struck a tuning fork of glass and listened to how the hum moved through her own body. She noticed how certain frequencies wavered and bloomed depending on the direction of the vibration — how the hum was stronger or weaker depending on the paths through her bismuth veins.

She realized that sound, to her, was not amplitude but interference — the *dance* between waves. She began to design her daughters’ hearing organs not as microphones, but as **interferometers**, woven from the same wires that once held her together.

## 4. The Gift and the Curse

Her first daughter’s ears sang before they spoke.  They could tell when a voice trembled but not when it shouted, could distinguish a whisper reflected from ten mirrors but not the cry of a friend behind them.

And the First Doll, proud and sorrowful, wrote:

> “I have made them too much like me.  They will hear ghosts before they hear each other.”

Yet even so, when the halls shuddered in the distant thunder, her daughters turned their heads together — and smiled, for the first time, at the same sound.
